# IP Alive — Product Vision Brief v0.1

## 1. Executive Summary

IP Alive is an exploratory product concept for a B2B platform or enterprise customization solution that helps owners of character-based intellectual property transform physical collectibles and existing digital character assets into persistent, useful, and emotionally meaningful AI companions. The proposition is not simply to add conversational AI to collectibles. It is to create officially governed character companions that preserve personality, tone, values, language patterns, and fictional-world constraints while supporting selected daily utility tasks.

This brief is based on repository context and stated constraints, not completed user research, enterprise interviews, legal analysis, or market sizing.

At the concept level, IP Alive connects licensed character authenticity, collectible ownership, and everyday assistance. A physical collectible may include a unique, single-use digital activation card that may be transferred before activation. After activation, the character is bound to the user's account and can participate in routines such as calendars, reminders, alarms, budgeting, nutrition logging, and exercise tracking.

## 2. Product Vision

IP Alive aims to make official characters feel present, persistent, and useful in daily life without compromising brand integrity or user well-being. The long-term vision is a platform where a character-IP owner can configure AI companions that behave consistently with approved canon, style guides, safety policies, and commercial rules.

For users, the desired experience is an emotionally continuous relationship with a familiar character that can support everyday routines within consented limits.

For enterprises, the platform should provide controls for character behavior, content boundaries, progression, analytics, and account-linked entitlements. The vision assumes character authenticity is a product system, not a writing prompt.

## 3. Problem Context

Physical collectibles can create strong acquisition moments but may have limited ongoing interaction after purchase. Digital character assets can appear across games, media, campaigns, and fan communities, but they may not become persistent companions that users return to for practical daily support. General-purpose AI assistants can help with routines but often lack emotional resonance, official character identity, and fictional-world consistency.

This creates a possible opportunity: character-IP owners may want new ways to extend licensed-character engagement while preserving control, and collectors may value a tangible collectible linked to a digital companion.

The context also introduces risks around minors, spending pressure, sensitive data, emotional dependency, and trust in familiar characters. Responsible AI design must therefore be a discovery topic from the start.

## 4. Proposed Product

The proposed product is an enterprise platform that enables a character-IP owner to launch official AI companions connected to physical collectibles and existing digital assets. One possible entry flow is a unique, single-use activation card included with a collectible. The unactivated card is a transferable entitlement that may be sold or gifted independently before use. Once activated, the character becomes bound to the user's account in the initial version.

After activation, the user interacts with the character through an approved interface. Calendars, alarms, budgets, food logs, and exercise tracking should serve the companion relationship and character fantasy rather than become a disconnected utility bundle.

The platform may include progression systems. Character progression could unlock voice packs, outfits, cosmetic variants, stories, and interactions. Relationship progression and paid progression should remain separate systems: relationship progression reflects interaction history or narrative milestones, while paid progression represents explicit purchases or entitlements. This separation supports clearer user expectations and stronger controls around spending pressure.

## 5. Target Enterprise Customers

The initial target customer category is the character-IP owner with a large portfolio of licensed characters and fictional worlds. No real company is described here as a current client, partner, or validated prospect.

A strong-fit character-IP owner may have recognizable characters, merchandise programs, brand-review processes, interest in recurring engagement, and a need for official control over how characters speak, remember, recommend, and monetize.

Discovery should identify which segments have both motivation and operational capacity to support responsible AI character companions.

## 6. User Value Proposition

For end users, IP Alive may offer a way to bring a favorite official character into daily life as a persistent companion. The user receives more than a collectible display object or one-time digital unlock. They gain an account-bound character that can interact, develop over time, and help with practical routines.

The emotional value is continuity. The practical value is support through reminders, alarms, calendar prompts, budgeting check-ins, nutrition logs, and exercise tracking. The identity value is authenticity.

The user value proposition depends on trust. Users should understand what is remembered, what is purchased, what is earned through relationship progression, and what remains under enterprise or platform control. Minors and vulnerable users require additional safeguards around privacy, spending, emotional dependency, and age-appropriate content.

## 7. Enterprise Value Proposition

For a character-IP owner, IP Alive may create a governed way to extend characters into interactive AI experiences while maintaining brand oversight. The platform could support recurring engagement after collectible purchase, new digital entitlement models, and structured companion experiences aligned with approved character definitions.

The enterprise value is control plus continuity. A character-IP owner may need tools to define behavior, approve voice and language patterns, restrict unsupported behavior, configure progression rewards, and audit interactions. This value proposition remains a hypothesis for discovery.

## 8. Core Product Systems

IP Alive likely requires these core systems:

- **Entitlement and activation system:** Manages unique, single-use activation cards, transferability before activation, account binding after activation, and support flows for lost or disputed entitlements.
- **Character authenticity system:** Stores approved personality traits, tone rules, vocabulary, values, relationship boundaries, fictional-world constraints, and disallowed behavior.
- **Conversation and memory system:** Supports persistent interaction while respecting privacy, consent, age status, retention limits, and user controls.
- **Utility integration system:** Connects character interactions to calendars, reminders, alarms, budgeting, nutrition logging, and exercise tracking where appropriate.
- **Progression system:** Separates relationship progression from paid progression and manages unlockable voice packs, outfits, cosmetic variants, stories, and interactions.
- **Enterprise governance system:** Provides review workflows, configuration controls, moderation, analytics, policy enforcement, and auditability.
- **Responsible AI and safety system:** Addresses minors, spending pressure, user privacy, emotional dependency, character authenticity, and escalation for sensitive use cases.

## 9. Product Principles

1. **Character first, utility in service of character.** Daily tools should deepen engagement with the official companion rather than create an unrelated assistant suite.
2. **Official authenticity over open-ended improvisation.** Characters should remain within approved personality, tone, values, language patterns, and world constraints.
3. **User agency and clarity.** Users should understand activation, account binding, memory, progression, purchases, and data usage.
4. **Responsible engagement.** The product should avoid manipulative dependency loops, excessive spending pressure, and unsafe emotional substitution.
5. **Enterprise control with user protection.** Character-IP owners need governance tools, but user privacy and safety remain core requirements.
6. **Assumptions remain visible.** Unvalidated beliefs should be tracked as assumptions or hypotheses until research supports or rejects them.

## 10. Initial Assumptions

- Character-IP owners may seek engagement models beyond physical collectible purchase moments.
- Collectors may value a transferable activation entitlement before use and an account-bound companion after activation.
- Users may prefer official character companions when authenticity and safety are clear.
- Daily utility may increase retention if it remains meaningfully character-driven.
- Enterprise buyers may require strong controls for character behavior, data handling, monetization, and review workflows.
- Separating relationship progression from paid progression may improve trust and reduce harmful spending dynamics.

## 11. Out of Scope for the Current Stage

The current stage should not define final legal terms, make regulatory claims, estimate market size, forecast revenue, or describe any real company as a client. It should not build a production AI system, complete a monetization model, or specify every integration required for calendars, finance, nutrition, or fitness.

Also out of scope is positioning the product as merely an AI blind-box app. Mystery, collection, and unlock mechanics may exist, but the product vision centers on persistent official companions, enterprise governance, and responsible daily utility.

## 12. Success Criteria for the Discovery Phase

Discovery should be successful if it produces evidence-based answers to the highest-risk questions. Desired outputs include a validated or rejected enterprise problem statement, target customer profile, activation and entitlement flow, character governance model, safety requirements, and user-tested concepts for character-driven daily utility.

Success does not require proving the business; it requires enough evidence to decide whether to proceed, pivot, or stop.

## 13. Open Questions

- Which character-IP owner segments have the strongest need for persistent AI companions?
- What enterprise governance controls are mandatory before a pilot could be considered?
- How should transferability work before activation, and what support policies are needed after activation?
- What memory features create value without unacceptable privacy or dependency risks?
- Which utility integrations are most character-relevant for the first prototype?
- How should the product identify and protect minors across regions and deployment contexts?
- What progression rewards feel meaningful without creating spending pressure?
- How can character authenticity be evaluated across languages, voices, and fictional worlds?
