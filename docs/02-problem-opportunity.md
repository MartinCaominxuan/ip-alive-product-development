# Problem and Opportunity

## Purpose

Separate preliminary consumer evidence from the broader product and enterprise hypotheses that still require validation.

## Project Status Note

This project is private, exploratory, and not affiliated with any named IP company. Any references to real companies should be treated only as hypothetical examples or future case-study candidates, not clients or partners.

## Guiding Questions

- What pain points might IP owners face after a collectible purchase?
- What daily-life use cases could justify continued character engagement?
- What risks arise if companionship, spending, and brand loyalty overlap?

## Evidence now available

A 165-response convenience survey included 150 respondents who had purchased a blind box or similar mystery collectible. Within the purchaser subset:

- recalled emotional connection averaged 5.19 immediately after unboxing and 4.12 at survey time;
- 39.33% described the connection as weaker, while 22.67% described it as stronger;
- among the 59 respondents who noticed a clear decline, 79.66% first noticed it within one month;
- selective memory, continuing content, and official-character consistency were the most selected connection-supporting features;
- character inconsistency and additional costs were the most selected concerns, both at 48.00%.

The evidence supports a post-unboxing connection-decay problem for a substantial subgroup and provides direction for prototype testing. It does not establish prevalence in the wider market or demonstrate that a digital character prevents decline. Full methods, denominators, and limitations are in [Blind-Box Connection Survey — Preliminary Results](../research/blind-box-connection-survey-results.md).

## Remaining hypotheses

- A well-governed digital extension may lengthen meaningful engagement with a physical collectible.
- Officially licensed character fidelity may increase trust relative to generic character chat.
- Selective memory and continuing authored content may matter more than social-community features.
- A physical-product-inclusive or one-time-fee model may fit preferences better than a recurring subscription.
- IP owners may value a governed post-purchase engagement platform enough to pilot or acquire it.

## Evidence still required

- de-identified row-level survey data and complete open-text responses;
- observed usability and repeat-use behavior with the prototype;
- longitudinal evidence of connection or retention;
- interviews with IP owners, licensors, merchandise operators, and content-review teams;
- willingness-to-pay experiments rather than stated preference alone;
- accessibility research with disabled participants.
