# Blind-Box Connection Survey — Preliminary Results

## Status and purpose

This document records a reproducible descriptive summary of the survey export received on 2026-08-28. It replaces earlier placeholder language that said results had not yet been supplied.

The findings are preliminary product-research evidence. They are not a representative market estimate, a causal test, a clinical result, or proof that IP Alive will improve well-being or retention.

## Source and denominators

- Survey platform: Wenjuanxing online questionnaire.
- Total submitted and consenting responses: **165**.
- Respondents who reported buying a blind box or similar mystery collectible for themselves: **150 (90.91%)**.
- Most purchase-experience and digital-extension questions were asked only of those 150 purchasers, so their denominator is **n=150**, not n=165.
- The timing-of-decline question was asked only of respondents who reported a clear decline, so its denominator is **n=59**.
- Demographic and fandom-participation items used **n=165**.

Percentages below are calculated within the applicable valid-response denominator. Multiple-response questions can total more than 100%.

## Descriptive findings

### Purchasing and current activity

- 77 of 165 respondents (46.67%) had purchased within the previous three months.
- Among purchasers, 73 of 150 (48.67%) had bought 2–10 items in total; 43 of 150 (28.67%) had bought more than 20.
- Current purchasing was generally infrequent: 60 of 150 (40.00%) bought every few months and 42 of 150 (28.00%) no longer purchased.
- The most selected purchase motivations were liking the character or IP (66.00%), visual/decorative appeal (56.00%), and gifting (40.00%). Resale or investment value was selected by 2.67%.

The combined 68.00% who purchase every few months or no longer purchase indicates low purchasing frequency in this sample. It does **not** by itself prove that these users lack continuing attachment to a collectible.

### Connection after unboxing

- Mean reported emotional connection was **5.19** immediately after unboxing and **4.12** at the time of the survey, a descriptive difference of **1.07 points**.
- Compared with unboxing, 59 of 150 respondents (39.33%) described their connection as weaker, 34 (22.67%) as stronger, 44 (29.33%) as approximately unchanged, and 13 (8.67%) as absent from the beginning.
- In a separate item, 59 of 150 (39.33%) said they had clearly noticed interest or connection begin to fade; 55 (36.67%) said it had not faded, 20 (13.33%) said they never felt connected, and 16 (10.67%) were unsure.
- Among the 59 respondents who noticed decline, 47 (79.66%) first noticed it within one month of opening. The most common single response was 1–2 weeks (22.03%).

These results support a **post-unboxing connection-decay problem for a substantial subgroup**, not the stronger claim that most collectors inevitably lose interest.

### Interest in a persistent digital extension

- Mean interest in an official physical-to-digital character experience for a liked character was **6.89** on the survey's slider measure.
- Features most often selected as likely to maintain connection were:
  - remembering user-selected past interactions: 43.33%;
  - new stories, dialogue, or activities appearing over time or progression: 42.00%;
  - maintaining the official character personality: 36.00%;
  - routines, learning, or practical utilities: 30.00%.
- Community interaction with other owners or fans was selected by 9.33%.

The strongest preferences align with IP Alive's persistent memory controls, authored progression, character-governance system, and optional daily-life tools. This is product-direction evidence, not evidence that the current prototype has already satisfied those needs.

### Trust, character fidelity, and service continuity

The most selected reasons for discomfort or reduced willingness to use were:

- inconsistency with the official character personality: 48.00%;
- additional subscription or in-app costs: 48.00%;
- repetitive or artificial interaction: 38.67%;
- the digital service shutting down and the character disappearing: 33.33%;
- excessive notifications or attempts to create dependence: 32.00%;
- unclear data collection or privacy practices: 21.33%.

These findings strengthen the case for canon-first generation, output evaluation, user-controlled memory and notifications, clear pricing, export/continuity planning, and explicit anti-dependency rules.

### Activation and payment preferences

- 40.67% preferred connection to begin at unboxing.
- 32.00% preferred activation only when they actively chose it.
- 9.33% did not want such a connection.
- 37.33% believed the digital experience should be included in the collectible price.
- 28.00% accepted a small one-time fee and 16.67% accepted a higher one-time fee if the experience remained permanently available.
- Only 4.00% selected a low monthly subscription.

The prototype should therefore treat physical-product redemption and explicit user activation as complementary entry routes. A recurring subscription should not be assumed to be the preferred business model.

## Sample description and limitations

- Women represented 68.48% of the total sample and respondents aged 18–22 represented 55.15%.
- Recruitment method, response rate, sampling frame, country distribution, and nonresponse pattern are not established in the aggregate export.
- The sample is therefore a convenience sample and should not be generalized to all collectors or all markets.
- Several subgroup analyses in the automatically generated report use very small cells. Descriptive percentage differences must not be called statistically significant without an appropriate test, uncertainty interval, and adequate sample size.
- The consent text said that continuing indicated the participant was at least 18, but six respondents selected “under 18.” This is a protocol and data-quality inconsistency. Adult-only reporting requires a documented exclusion decision using row-level data.
- The aggregate document does not include individual-level records or the full open-text responses. Missingness, paired tests, multivariable analyses, reliable qualitative coding, and independent reproduction are therefore not yet possible.
- The immediate and current connection measures were collected retrospectively in one cross-sectional survey. Recall effects, recency, current storage, and time since purchase may influence the ratings.
- Interest and willingness-to-pay responses are stated preferences, not observed adoption or purchasing behavior.

## Claims permitted by the current evidence

Use:

> In this convenience sample, emotional connection was lower at the time of the survey than respondents recalled immediately after unboxing, and 39.33% of purchasers explicitly described their connection as weaker. Respondents showed strongest interest in selective memory, continuing content, and character fidelity, while also expressing substantial concerns about character inconsistency, added costs, repetitive interaction, dependency-oriented notifications, and service discontinuation.

Do not use:

- “Most blind-box owners lose interest after purchase.”
- “The survey proves IP Alive will increase retention.”
- “Users will pay for AI companions.”
- “Digital characters reduce anxiety or replace unstable human relationships.”
- “Demographic groups differ significantly,” unless this is later supported by appropriate statistical analysis.

## Public-repository data policy

The repository publishes this aggregate, de-identified summary rather than the original platform export or automatically generated AI report. Source exports should remain in controlled research storage until consent scope, privacy, eligibility, and redistribution rights have been reviewed. An AI-generated interpretation is not a primary research artifact and should not be presented as independently verified analysis.
