# Academic Research Connection

## Associated honors study

**Waiting Together: A Feasibility and Preliminary Outcomes Study of a Four-Session ACL™ Dyadic Intervention for College Students Facing Academic and Career Uncertainty**

The honors project examines the feasibility and preliminary outcomes of a structured four-session dyadic intervention for college students experiencing academic and career uncertainty. Its central interest is whether a stable relational process may be associated with reduced distress or improved coping in the face of uncertainty.

## Connection to IP Alive

IP Alive asks a related but distinct design question: if a physical collectible normally produces a brief acquisition experience, could a persistent digital character create continuing engagement and participate in everyday routines?

The projects connect through four themes:

1. uncertainty and the desire for continuity;
2. repeated relational processes rather than one-time encounters;
3. stability and predictability as possible design mechanisms;
4. feasibility and safety before efficacy claims.

## Reflexive position

The creator experienced two major interpersonal traumas. Those experiences shaped skepticism about the apparent stability of human relationships and curiosity about whether an AI-mediated relationship can feel more predictable because availability, memory, and character boundaries can be designed.

This is a source of research motivation, not evidence that human stability is false or that human–AI relationships are inherently superior or healthier. A system can be technically consistent while emotionally asymmetric, commercially controlled, interruptible, and vulnerable to model, policy, licensing, or company changes.

A defensible research proposition is:

> Under clearly disclosed and responsibly bounded conditions, a persistent digital character may provide perceived continuity and predictable interaction; whether that continuity reduces uncertainty-related distress, extends post-purchase engagement, or creates new harms requires empirical study.

## Blind-box research link

The planned survey examines whether users perceive limited continuing experience after acquiring a blind-box collectible and whether they value a digital extension. The dataset has not yet been provided. Until analysis is complete, statements about “most users” are provisional and must not include invented percentages.

When data arrive, analysis should distinguish:

- frequency and duration of post-purchase engagement;
- collectors versus occasional buyers;
- interest in digital characters, memory, mail, games, utilities, and unlocks;
- privacy, spending, accessibility, and emotional-dependency concerns;
- willingness to use versus willingness to pay;
- demographic differences where sample size permits;
- open-text responses that challenge the hypothesis.

## Non-equivalence

The honors intervention involves human dyads and a structured intervention. IP Alive involves a human and a designed character system. They differ in reciprocity, agency, mechanism, ethics, and evidence. IP Alive should be presented as an adjacent design exploration, not a direct application or demonstrated outcome of the honors study.

## Future questions

- Does persistent interaction extend engagement with a physical collectible?
- Which components drive return behavior?
- Does predictability feel supportive, repetitive, uncanny, or manipulative?
- How do users distinguish companionship, entertainment, utility, and reciprocity?
- Do disability, trauma, loneliness, or uncertainty experiences alter perceived benefits and risks?
- What safeguards reduce dependency without destroying continuity?
- How does strict canon fidelity affect existing-IP fans' trust?

## Claims discipline

Until supported, use “hypothesizes,” “explores,” “participants in this sample reported,” or “preliminary results suggest.” Do not say “proves,” “reduces anxiety,” “AI relationships are more stable,” or “most collectors want this” without evidence.
