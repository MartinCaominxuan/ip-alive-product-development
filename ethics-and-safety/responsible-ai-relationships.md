# Responsible AI Relationships

## Position

IP Alive explores whether a persistent character can provide perceived continuity without presenting that character as conscious, reciprocal, or a replacement for human support. Technical predictability is not relational mutuality.

## Required boundaries

- Identify the experience as artificial when confusion is reasonably possible.
- Never claim consciousness, human embodiment, suffering caused by absence, or exclusive ownership of the user.
- Do not punish disengagement or imply abandonment.
- Keep paid progression and relationship progression distinguishable.
- Do not discourage human relationships, professional care, or emergency assistance.
- Production memory must be visible, correctable, exportable, and deletable.
- Sensitive advice must remain inside approved character and safety boundaries.
- Existing IP must remain inside licensor-approved canon and narrative scope.

## Stability hypothesis

A governed character may feel more predictable because availability, memory rules, and behavior constraints can be designed. That may be supportive for some users, but can also create dependency, avoidance, grief after service changes, or misplaced trust. The project must investigate benefits and harms.

## Trauma-informed caution

The creator's interpersonal-trauma history is reflexive motivation, not a universal account of relationships. Research involving trauma, loneliness, uncertainty, or attachment requires careful recruitment, consent, distress procedures, debriefing, and institutional guidance.

## Escalation and validation

Future production needs escalation for self-harm, abuse, medical, legal, financial, and crisis content. Character roleplay must never obstruct urgent safety responses. Longitudinal research should examine continuity, dependency, artificiality understanding, service discontinuation, and differences by age, disability, loneliness, trauma history, and collecting intensity.
